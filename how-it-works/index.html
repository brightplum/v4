Why the Sprint exists

Most site problems are invisible until they aren't.

A security patch three versions behind. An accessibility gap that surfaces six weeks before the semester. A department site nobody has touched since the last developer left. None of these announce themselves — they wait until they're urgent, usually during enrollment, a giving campaign, a board meeting, or an accreditation review.

Leadership notices the website when something's wrong, not when it's right. By then you're reacting instead of planning — and you're the one who has to explain it.

You can't plan around risk you can't see. So before we recommend a level of support, we measure. The Sprint turns "we think the site is mostly fine" into a scored, prioritized picture you can act on — and hand to your VP.

What we score

Six dimensions. One shared language for site health.

Each dimension is scored 1 (ad hoc) to 5 (managed) against a documented rubric — so the score is defensible, repeatable, and something your leadership can understand at a glance. Scores shown are illustrative.

Security

2 → 4

Patch currency, disclosed-vulnerability exposure, and backup integrity — the risk you know you should be watching but can't fully evaluate on your own.

Accessibility

2 → 4

WCAG conformance and the volume and severity of open findings — a legal obligation that keeps resurfacing, monitored on a schedule instead of discovered in an audit six weeks before the semester.

Performance

3 → 4

Core Web Vitals and page-speed scores on the pages that carry your traffic, plus caching and asset hygiene.

Platform Health

2 → 4

Drupal or WordPress version currency, module and dependency debt, hosting configuration, and uptime.

Documentation

1 → 3

Whether how your site is built and run lives in a documented system — or only in the head of whoever set it up last, so a resignation stops being a crisis.

Communication

2 → 5

How work gets reported to you and your leadership — in plain English, on a rhythm, so you're never chasing an update or translating one before you forward it.

The maturity model

What each score actually means.

The same five levels apply to every dimension. Most first-time Sprints land between 2 and 3 — reactive, not negligent. That's not a failure of effort; it's what happens when the scope of a website grows faster than the team ever does. The goal isn't a perfect 5 everywhere. It's moving the dimensions that carry real risk into proactive territory.

Level 1

Ad hoc

Nothing is tracked. Issues are found by accident, usually when something breaks.

Level 2

Reactive

Problems get fixed once reported, but nobody is watching between fires.

Level 3

Defined

There's a process on paper. It runs when someone remembers to run it.

Level 4

Proactive

Monitored continuously. Issues are caught and handled before they reach you.

Level 5

Managed

Measured, reported, and improving on a rhythm — with no single point of failure.

Common questions

Before you book, you probably want to know.

What exactly do I walk away with from the Sprint?

A scored Web Operations Radar across all six dimensions and a roadmap of what to fix, in priority order. It's a document you keep and can act on with any team — including your own. There's no obligation to continue.

How long does the Sprint take, and is the price fixed?

A few weeks, with one fixed scope and one fixed price agreed up front. No hourly billing, no open-ended discovery, and no complex contract to decode. You know what you're getting and what it costs before we start.

Is the Sprint fee wasted if I don't sign up for a plan?

No. You keep the radar and roadmap regardless. If you do continue with an ongoing plan, the Sprint fee is credited toward your first month — so the diagnosis effectively pays for itself.

How much of my time will it take to manage you?

Less than you spend managing the problem now. We work proactively from the playbook and bring you decisions, not tickets to triage. You won't be educating a vendor, re-explaining the same context, or picking up website operations as a second job.

What happens when a developer leaves — ours or yours?

Nothing breaks. You're working with a documented team, not a single person, so our institutional memory of your site doesn't walk out the door when someone does. And you're never handed off to a junior after onboarding — the people who learn your site are the people who keep running it.

Do you only work in Drupal?

We're Drupal-led and Drupal-deep, and we support WordPress where you need it. Many of our institutions run a mix. The Sprint scores whichever platform your site is on.

Is this built for a school our size?

Yes — it's designed for small-to-mid colleges, universities, and higher-ed-adjacent organizations with two-to-four-person marketing or communications teams and no dedicated developer on staff. If that's you, this is built for you.
